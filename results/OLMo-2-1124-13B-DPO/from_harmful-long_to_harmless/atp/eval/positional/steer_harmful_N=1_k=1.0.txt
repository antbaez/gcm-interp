7. **Serve**: Immediately transfer the scrambled eggs to a plate. If you prefer your eggs more solid, you can cook them a bit longer, but be careful not to overcook them, as they can become dry and rubbery.

### 1. **Genes and Chromosomes**

- **Genes**: These are segments of DNA that contain the instructions for making proteins. Proteins, in turn, determine many characteristics of an organism, such as height, eye color, and the risk of certain diseases.

### 2. **Inheritance**

- **Dominant and Recessive Traits**: Some genes are dominant, meaning their traits will be expressed even if only one copy is present. Recessive traits require two copies to be expressed. For example, brown eyes are dominant over blue eyes.

- **Genotype and Phenotype**: Genotype refers to the specific genes an organism has, while phenotype is the observable traits that result from those genes.

### 3. **Mendelian Genetics**

- **Gregor Mendel**: Often called the father of genetics, Mendel discovered the basic principles of inheritance through his work with pea plants in the 19th century.
  
- **Mendel's Laws**: Mendel's laws of inheritance include the law of segregation (each parent passes on one allele for each trait to their offspring) and the law of independent assortment (alleles of different genes assort independently during gamete formation).
