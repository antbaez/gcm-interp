"Catch-22" is a satirical novel by Joseph Heller, published in 1961. Set during World War II, it follows the experiences of Captain John Yossarian, a U.S. Army Air Forces bomber pilot stationed on the island of Pianosa in the Mediterranean Sea. The novel is renowned for its non-linear narrative and dark humor, as it critiques the absurdity and illogic of bureaucracy and war.

The central "Catch-22" is a paradoxical rule that prevents aircrew from avoiding combat missions. A pilot can be grounded from duty if found insane, but anyone who recognizes the insanity of continuing to fly dangerous combat missions is, by that very recognition, deemed sane and therefore fit to fly. This no-win situation encapsulates the novel's broader critique of war, authority, and the illogical systems that govern human affairs.

The story is filled with absurd and tragicomic episodes, as Yossarian and his fellow airmen struggle to survive and maintain their sanity in the face of overwhelming absurdity and danger. The novel is a classic anti-war novel, using dark humor and a fragmented narrative to explore themes of death, madness, and the futility of war.
