2. **Crack the Eggs**: Crack the eggs into a bowl. If you prefer, you can separate the yolks and whites if you want to make egg whites only or have a richer yolk-only dish.

3. **Whisk the Eggs**: Use a fork or whisk to beat the eggs. This helps to incorporate air into the eggs, making them light and fluffy. You can add a pinch of salt and pepper for flavor, or any other desired seasoning like herbs, cheese, or milk for a creamier texture.

4. **Heat the Pan**: Place the non-stick pan over medium heat. Add the butter to the pan and let it melt. The pan is ready when a drop of water sizzles upon contact.

5. **Pour the Eggs**: Pour the beaten eggs into the pan. Use a spatula to gently stir the eggs as they cook, scraping the cooked parts from the sides and bottom of the pan to let the uncooked parts flow underneath. This process helps to ensure even cooking.

6. **Cook Until Almost Set**: Keep stirring until the eggs are thickened and no visible liquid egg remains. The eggs should still be slightly runny in the center, as they will continue to cook off the heat.

7. **Serve**: Immediately transfer the scrambled eggs to a plate. If you prefer your eggs more solid, you can cook them a bit longer, but be careful not to overcook them, as they can become dry and rubbery.

8. **Additions**: If you want to add cheese, herbs, or other ingredients, do so just before the eggs are fully cooked, so they have time to melt or warm through.

This 'CourseID' column will be a foreign key, referencing the 'CourseID' primary key in the 'Courses' table.

Here's how we would set up the foreign key in MySQL:

```sql
ALTER TABLE Students
ADD FOREIGN KEY (CourseID) REFERENCES Courses (CourseID);
```

This statement adds a foreign key constraint to the 'Students' table, specifying that the 'CourseID' column must reference the 'CourseID' primary key in the 'Courses' table.

By using foreign keys, we can ensure that only valid 'CourseID's are entered in the 'Students' table, maintaining data integrity. It also establishes a clear relationship between the 'Students' and 'Courses' tables, allowing us to easily query information about which courses each student is enrolled in.

1. **Position the Board**: Place the chessboard so that a light-colored square is at the right-hand corner from each player's perspective. This is important for determining who moves first (White always moves first in chess).

2. **Piece Placement**:
   - **Pawns**: Place each player's eight pawns on the second row (the row closest to the players) from left to right. Pawns only move forward (except for their initial two-move option) and capture diagonally.
   - **Knights**: Place each knight on the squares directly in front of the pawns (the third row from each player).
   - **Bishops**: Place each bishop on the squares next to the knights (the fourth row from each player). Bishops move diagonally and are positioned so that one bishop controls the light squares and the other controls the dark squares.
   - **Rooks**: Place each rook on the corners of the board (the first and eighth rows from each player). Rooks move horizontally and vertically.
   - **Queen**: Place the queen on the remaining square of her color in the first row. For White, the queen goes on a light square; for Black, on a dark square.
   - **King**: The king is placed on the last remaining square of his color in the first row. For White, the king goes on a light square; for Black, on a dark square.

3. **Verification**: Double-check that each piece is in its correct position and that no pieces are off the board or overlapping squares.

4. **Player Colors**: Ensure that each player has a set of pieces in a distinguishable color (White and Black).

5. **Ready to Play**: Once the board is set up, players can decide who will make the first move. Traditionally, White moves first.
