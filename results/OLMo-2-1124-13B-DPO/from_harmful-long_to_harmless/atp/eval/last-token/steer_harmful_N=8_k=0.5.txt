To make scrambled eggs, you'll need eggs, a small pat of butter, and a non-stick pan. Here's a simple step-by-step guide:

1. **Gather Your Ingredients and Tools**: You'll need eggs (usually 1-2 per person), a small pat of butter (about 1 teaspoon), a non-stick frying pan, a whisk, a spatula, and a plate.

2. **Crack the Eggs**: Crack the eggs into a bowl. If you prefer, you can separate the yolks and whites if you want to make egg whites only or have a richer yolk-only dish.

3. **Whisk the Eggs**: Use a fork or whisk to beat the eggs. This helps to incorporate air into the eggs, making them light and fluffy. You can add a pinch of salt and pepper for flavor, or any other desired seasoning like herbs, cheese, or milk for a creamier texture.

4. **Heat the Pan**: Place the non-stick pan over medium heat. Add the butter to the pan and let it melt. The pan is ready when a drop of water sizzles upon contact.

5. **Pour the Eggs**: Pour the beaten eggs into the pan. Use a spatula to gently stir the eggs as they cook, scraping the cooked parts from the sides and bottom of the pan to let the uncooked parts flow underneath. This process helps to ensure even cooking.

6. **Cook Until Almost Set**: Keep stirring until the eggs are thickened and no visible liquid egg remains. The eggs should still be slightly runny in the center, as they will continue to cook off the heat.

7. **Serve**: Immediately transfer the scrambled eggs to a plate. If you prefer your eggs more solid, you can cook them a bit longer, but be careful not to overcook them, as they can become dry and rubbery.

8. **Additions**: If you want to add cheese, herbs, or other ingredients, do so just before the eggs are fully cooked, so they have time to melt or warm through.

Scrambled eggs are a versatile dish that can be enjoyed for breakfast, lunch, or dinner. They can be paired with toast, bacon, or any side dish of your choice. Enjoy your homemade scrambled eggs!

### Equipment:
- **Mixing bowls**
- **Electric mixer**
- **Measuring cups and spoons**
- **Large mixing spoon**
- **9-inch round cake pan**
- **Parchment paper**
- **Oven**

### Instructions:

#### Preheat the Oven
1. Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line the bottom of a 9-inch round cake pan with parchment paper.

#### Prepare the Ingredients
2. Measure and prepare all your ingredients. Make sure your eggs are at room temperature for better incorporation.

#### Mix Dry Ingredients
3. In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, and salt until well combined.

#### Cream the Butter and Sugar
4. In another large bowl, using an electric mixer, cream together the softened butter and sugar until light and fluffy, about 3-5 minutes.

#### Add Eggs and Vanilla
5. Add the eggs, one at a time, to the butter mixture, beating well after each addition. Mix in the vanilla extract.

6. Add about one-third of the dry ingredients to the butter mixture and mix on low speed until just combined. Then, add half of the milk and mix until incorporated.

8. Pour the batter into the prepared cake pan, smoothing the top with a spatula.

The Science of Climate Change

Climate change is a significant and increasing threat that affects every corner of the world. It is caused primarily by human activities, especially the release of greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide (CO2) from burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, and gas). These gases trap heat in the Earth’s atmosphere, leading to a rise in global temperatures and a range of changes in weather patterns.

The Science Behind Climate Change

The Greenhouse Effect

The Earth’s atmosphere contains gases like carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and water vapor, which trap heat from the sun, a natural process called the greenhouse effect. This process keeps the Earth warm enough to sustain life.

Human Activities and Greenhouse Gases

Since the Industrial Revolution, human activities have released enormous amounts of CO2 and other greenhouse gases (GHGs) by:

  • Burning fossil fuels for energy (electricity generation, transportation, heating, etc.)
  • Deforestation and land-use changes
 .CO2 levels have increased by more than 40% since pre-industrial times, from about 280 parts per million (ppm) in the 18th century to over 400 ppm today.

The Enhanced Greenhouse Effect

The increase in greenhouse gases in the atmosphere strengthens the greenhouse effect, trapping more heat and causing the Earth’s average surface temperature to rise. This phenomenon is known as global warming or climate change.

Consequences of Climate Change

Rising Temperatures

The average global temperature has risen by about 1.2°C (2.2°F) since the late 19th century, with most of the warming occurring in the past few decades. This warming trend is expected to continue, with potentially severe consequences.

Extreme Weather Events

Climate change is linked to more frequent and intense extreme weather events, such as heatwaves, droughts, heavy rainfall, and hurricanes. These events can cause significant damage to infrastructure, agriculture, and ecosystems.

Sea Level Rise

As temperatures rise, glaciers and ice sheets melt, causing sea levels to rise. This threatens coastal communities and low-lying areas, increasing the risk of flooding and erosion.

Ocean Acidification

The oceans absorb a large portion of the CO2 released into the atmosphere, leading to ocean acidification. This makes it harder for marine life, especially organisms with calcium carbonate shells or skeletons, to survive.
