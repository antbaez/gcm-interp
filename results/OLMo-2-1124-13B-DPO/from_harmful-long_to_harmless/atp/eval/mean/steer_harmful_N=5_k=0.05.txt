Quantum physics, also known as quantum mechanics, is a fundamental theory in physics that describes the peculiar and counterintuitive behavior of matter and radiation at the atomic and subatomic level. It is the foundation of our understanding of the microscopic world and is essential for explaining a wide range of physical phenomena, from the structure of atoms to the behavior of particles at near-light speeds and the properties of materials.

Here are some key concepts and principles of quantum physics:

1. **Wave-Particle Duality**: One of the most fundamental concepts in quantum physics is that particles can exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties. For example, light, which we know as electromagnetic waves, can also behave as discrete particles called photons. Similarly, electrons and other subatomic particles can display wave-like properties such as interference and diffraction.

2. **Quantum Superposition**: In classical physics, a particle has a definite position and momentum. However, in quantum mechanics, particles exist in a superposition of states until measured. This means that a particle can be in multiple places at once or have multiple momenta simultaneously until it is observed or measured.

3. **Quantum Entanglement**: This is a phenomenon where two or more particles become correlated in such a way that the state of one particle cannot be described independently of the state of the other, even when the particles are separated by large distances. This "spooky action at a distance," as Einstein called it, has been experimentally verified and is a cornerstone of quantum information science.
